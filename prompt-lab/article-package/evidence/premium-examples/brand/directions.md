# Visual directions and source observations

These are original fictional commercial concepts. Public designer images are research references; their brand names, marks and compositions are not intended to be reproduced. The inspected references below supply specific principles, not evidence that prompting improves a model. Built-in image generation does not expose the backend model, seed, quality parameter or reproducible renderer version.

## 1. Print-led consumer identity

Source: [Pentagram — EBBS](https://www.pentagram.com/work/ebbs/story).

Visually inspected the portfolio's close-up of three labeled silver cans and its black-and-white neon sign. Useful observations: large type at the top, one high-contrast illustration between text levels, few competing elements, matte paper contrasted with reflective aluminum. OFFDAY uses a new name, coral/powder-blue/ultramarine palette, a new almond-and-circle emblem and different compositions. Merch, packaging and advertisement share this fictional identity.

## 2. Geometric pictogram family

Source: [IBM Design Language — Pictogram design](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/iconography/pictograms/design/).

Visually inspected the house/garage construction example and enlarged corner detail. Useful observations: consistent line weight, orthogonal geometry, selective exterior corner rounding, few small details. The original set depicts six listening/creative-work objects. It is a generated raster concept sheet, not IBM artwork, a production icon font, or verified grid-snapped vector geometry.

## 3. Flat editorial forms and negative space

Source: [Malika Favre — The Travel editions](https://www.malikafavre.com/collections/the-travel-editions).

Visually inspected the green foliage/portrait image used by the collection and the blue La Jungle print. Useful observations: restricted saturated flat colors, dramatic silhouette cropping, large dark masses, restrained highlight shapes and tiny warm accents. The original editorial scene changes the subject, composition and palette: a pianist seen from above whose piano lid and shadow become the central graphic. The logo is a separate simple one-color mark using negative space and geometric economy, with no borrowed subject matter.
